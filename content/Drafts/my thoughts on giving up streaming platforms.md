# Draft #1

Recently, I’ve been trying to improve my “media consumption” diet.

Big companies are fighting for your attention, your data, and your purchasing power, so it is an uphill battle to change media habits. Ultimately, I want to be more deliberate about my time and attention, and support artists and small businesses directly.

My thoughts here are primarily about music streaming but can be somewhat applied to film streaming services too. Film services are different in some ways. For this discussion particularly, film streaming platforms are very fractured in terms of content, while with music streaming the average consumer gets about the same selection on any major platform.

### What are you really giving up

###### discovery algorithms

Assuming you are a frequent user, if you were to ween yourself off music streaming, the major feature that you’d notice yourself missing is the recommendation/discovery algorithm for finding new artists. To me this is the major service these platforms provide, and depending on who you are, giving this up may be a deal breaker.

It's just fomo

It reduces potential for socialising

There is something generic about the recommendation algorithms

### What are the alternatives?

- buying
- piracy

###### supporting artists

Artists royalties on platforms like Spotify, Apple Music, and YouTube music are miniscule. On Spitify average revenue is $0.003-0.005 per stream, depending on your audiences location (subscription prices differ between countries). And that's before the label, managers, and distributors take their shares.

For context, buying a $20 CD would pay the same as 5000 streams. If you listened to that artist once a day it would take over 13 years for them to earn those $20.

In terms of ethics, and paying artists you care about fairly, then streaming is the worst thing you could do. Or is it?
