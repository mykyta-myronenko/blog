%%Relates to [[Industrial Revolution]], [[Sapiens; Yuval Noah Harari]], [[dualist thinking]]%%

Before industrial revolution domesticaton of animals was a very important to make human lives easier. Without the horse-power we could not possibly feed the large stationary^[non-nomadic] societies. But even before humans decided to settle and invest into agriculture—as opposed to staying as hunter-gatherers—horses played a part. ==What would have been if horses never existed?==

Humans see themselves as seperate from animals, valueing human life as more important. The subjective morality of this can be argued[^1], it's not surpirisng how badly we have treat other animals, when you look at how we've treat other humans. Our tribal 'us-and-them' brains, historically seem eager to excuse doing the wrong thing, which has surely made it easier to justify exploiting of *them* for the benefit of *us* without considerations of the exploited's subjective feelings. Some non-human animals had it better, than others—most had it not so great.

[^1]: Maybe we are more attached to other humans because we understand each other better. Some animals can feel like part of family while others are strangers, regardless of their species. There are utlilitarian arguments too; humans may provide more 'good' to the human species, and potentially all species (though mostly not)^[citation], so it is evolutionarily pragmatic to prioritise our own, for own interest, own benefit, own survival.

Alongside dogs (who I'd say were dealt the better hand in the athropocenic sphere)^[work on phrasing] horses were one of the first to be accepted (or coerced, depending on perspective) into the human culture. 

But even with the Indsustrial Revolution, that promised to reduce the labour by using the new technology, not much has changed for *us*. Agricultural work was replaced by factory work, and for many the hours increased^[citation needed, this is just a guess, I think I heard this somewhere].

Industrial Revolution did, however, make your average horses life a lot different. If we look from a perspective of evolution, with the wide adoption of cars, horses had lost huge stakes in the propagation of genes. Now that they were (mostly) out of the transportation and agriculture industires, what was left were vestigious^[citation needed, by marginal I mean not wide-spread, maybe change the wording] jobs like horse-racing, and mounted police force. To me it looks like, the horse in our culture remained as more of a symbol for posterity, a sort of commemoration to contribution horse ancestors have made to the human good.

Who is to say how our society would have developed without horses. At the very least we owe horses an unknowable amount of time that was saved by the rapid progress brought through sheer horse-power, horse-sweat and horse-blood. The ultimate outcome of horse labour could be the existence of billions of people, our interconnectedness, relative safety (but also millions of dead to war). Our technological, and scientific advances, space travel, medicine (but also slavery^[not sure if slavery would have been still present]). In any case we can't conciously put the blame for our faults on the backs of horses (they've carried enough *bud-um tis*). Thinking of what would have been if horses were nay, however, is *humbling*.
