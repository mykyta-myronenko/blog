Urban, landscape, architecture, interior designers and anyone adjacent, can you actually change the world? If you applied yourself to the single idea of making the biggest impact you possibly can what would it be?

I think it is possible and architecture is in a unique position. Environment has a direct impact on how we behave, how we feel, what we think and hence what we do.^[citations for each needed] %%examples%%

Places, tangible, controllable and creatable environments, things you can visualise and coordinate with the help of relatively small teams shape _us_. First, us as people and over time, us as species. We shape places, they shape us and we then shape the world. In that sense placemaking is a meta discipline that lets us change ourselves, by leveraging societal and cultural change (in theory).

What are the most practical examples of architecture having a positive social impact? I can think of Soviet mass housing that you could argue accidentally allowed more freedom of thought to its people. Schools are a clear example to me where you can make the environment quite literally mould people since kids are so mailable, but what is the actual impact a school building has on the students? Is the design significant, or does the mere accessibility to education trump any clever behavioural-psychology-driven design? The impact of a good teacher is huge on their students, what is the impact of a good building, and what _is_ a good building?
