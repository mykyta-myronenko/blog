You cannot design away the prison culture. Before you consider designing beautiful prisons, consider doing a project for a school, a university or a youth centre, oh and make the access to all those for free and solve income inequality if you have time left.

The main issues with prisons is not that they look bad, are not programmatically resolved or lack recreation. The issues with prisons are the disproportionate imprisonment rates for low-income communities, the culture of misusing power by police and prison staff, and inability to rehabilitate people after release.

Norway has a far lower recidivism rate than US. Norway also has more humanely designed prisons than US. This does not mean that Norwegian designers solved prisons and suddenly crime rates are down because inmates have been somehow enlightened by the beautiful Scandinavian design. What's more likely is that the attitude towards imprisonment in Norway is different, better even.

Of course the same solutions from Norway would not apply in US. The societal contexts completely different. US is more individualistic, more religious, more racially diverse (and has more baggage, like inequality and structural racism), and is a lot bigger. Norway is not as capitalist, it has free education (universities too), free medical care and a more aggressive welfare programme.

As mentioned above, a big reason for crime rates and being imprisoned is that many people engage in criminal activities from young age. Complex structural and cultural frameworks result in less stable households for low-income communities and youth will often look for a escape outside of their households. This is usually the streets which pose many dangers of assault, robbery, drugs, and also participation in criminal activities. It may start with just boredom or as a way of self-sustaining (i.e. selling drugs to have independence from an abusive household), but can often become a gateway into a culture that will push towards poor life choices and is also targeted by authorities.

The solution is to get youths of the streets. I can't pretend I know how, but some data^[citation needed check Freakonomics] shows that education that is free and good is helpful, that having strong reliable and stable communities is helpful, and that having job opportunities despite your background is helpful.

Architecture can't fix these issues but it at least can shift focus on the more impactful problems instead of repackaging prisons into looking more friendly.
