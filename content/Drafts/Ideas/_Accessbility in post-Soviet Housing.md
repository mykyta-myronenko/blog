This was the topic I wanted to focus for my uni architectural dissertation but my supervisor drove me away from it, which I don't appreciate.

I am still curious to explore if there are fundamental solutions to accessibility and how our environment could be imporoved to make things easier.

To begin with I would shift the attention to Kiev.

- [ ] public transit. How viable is it?
  - how difficult is the journey from here to the nearest hospital
  - how accessible is the nearest park
  - what does it take to get into the underground.
  - what metro stations meet accessibility standards
  - where is the closest supermaket that a wheelchair can fit through the door of, and person can get in without having to ask for help.
  - how easy is it to cross the road? do the roads have facilities for blind people
  - how well are busses equiped to allow a wheelchair user in?
  - how does the metro, buses and other places that need to communicate information dinamically accommodate deaf people
  - what are some of the most common Soviet housing units in Kiev, and how can they be transfomed to be more friendly for the less mobile people, or those with impaired senses. (or perhaps there is a less common typology that has room for a ramp)
  - what are the common quality of life features often missing in cities.
    - does kiev have any of these?
    - how difficult would be to implement these?

okay so less public now

- [ ] what does a disabled person do, if an old elevator in the building is broken?
  - [ ] what fail safes could be implemented to prevent something like this from happening?
- [ ] home dimensions that allow for wheelchair turns and access to shelves, plugs, washing etc. basically ways that architecture can provide automicity to wheelchair users.
- [ ] programes to include accessible housing quota for every renovated typology unit.
  - i'll break it down a bit. If there is a standardised solution that can be applied a large number of houses it would be good to implement it widely. We can place some of the accessible housing (of different kinds) on the ground floor (because by design ground floor is the most accessible since buildings are linear). We basically then need to only renovate the ground floor of the typology - great.
    - We include the rails that lead people to where they need, clear foot signal bump thingies, materiality differences, some audio signals, maybe motorised solutions for elevation changes (or perhaps there is a less common typology that has room for a ramp), maybe some interface that allows to call for help / ambulance.
    - The goal is not populate the whole ground floor with disabled people and design super bespoke housing that no one will use. It's fine to have 2-3 maybe apartments that are well designed, and have a variety of solutions that suit individuals with disability. Essentially you pick the areas in the city that make most sense (like the most accessible to public transit) and renovate some of the apartments, seqeusterign parts of the ground floor to people in need.
    - A workable alternative, that doesn't require having unclaimed apartments, in every building would be a having a sort of government-funded team that provides specific relatively-standard renovation services.
      - Okay, so for example, a wheelchair user signs up for the programme. After their spot in the queue comes, a team arrives and makes some gradual changes. They come every week do some progress and leave, while the apartment is worked on the wheelchair user is out. If they are working, then that's simple, if not, then there'd be a person who looks after them provided so that they can be assisted in doing stuff around the city maybe (dam this is tircky stuff actually). Anyway, the workers lower the kitchen shelves, they make the sinks easier to use, they change the bathtub / shower into something actually usable, make curtains easily accesible, elevated the washing machine maybe, add a drying rack, maybe replace the fridge, change light bulbs to LEDs so they last longer and need not replacing, they change the flooring for better traction if it has to be done. The point is, they make it more liveable. And I would also expect them giving contacts for emergencies as well as if something needs fixing. Idk, a curtain rails falls down or something; someone needs to come the next day and replace it (for free obviously).
