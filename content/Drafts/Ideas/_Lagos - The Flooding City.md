- What can be done on the government scale?
	- I think there is an architectural solution that can be made. But for this you first need to clear out space. This is not simple.
		1. You need a new place for people to move. Meaning probably some enw urban area that is better than where people lived before and subsidised to be affordable for them to move. There needs to be a good (preferrably positive) incentive.
		2. Get rid of the houses over a decently sized area so that the new development has some scale to it.
		3. Build the architectural solution. Which I'd think would be something that links to the drainage infrastructure, makes use of elevation. Has access to public transit (which seems to be quite decent). Includes some flash flood prevention chanels.
		4. You move people from a different area into the newly-developed zone.
		5. Repeat from Step 2, and slowly overtime you identify areas of most need, rebuild old areas, maintain new, and give people housing that they won't be moved out of. The economic strategy could conceptually hedge on reducing flood costs, by developing areas that are more resilient, so that overtime, as most of the cities problematic areas transform, you end up with less costs sunk (literally), plus a better quality of life.
