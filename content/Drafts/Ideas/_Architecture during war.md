How can an architect be useful at a time of war? #question I found my use in the organisation of information. Maybe in countries that (undeservedly) value architects more there are jobs that are more tightly linked to architecture.

## Konrad Wachsmann

Wachsmann wasn't American but German; he emigrated to the US in 1941 and collaborated with Gropius in the development of a modular prefab system, the Packaged House.[^1]

During wartime, he also designed a very interesting modular structure.[^2]

## Buckminster Fuller

Another well-known example of war architecture is the Dymaxion deployment unit of Buckminster Fuller; it was used in the Persian Gulf.[^3]

## Quonset Huts

Another interesting story is the one of the classic metal huts, officially called the Quonset Huts.[^4]

## The Eames during WW2

The Eames also made stuff for WWII - it's probably harder to apply (it's all made of steam-bent plywood) but still interesting to look at.[^5]

## Hiding Buildings

There is an article inside a book which is not so much about buildings but rather about hiding them.[^6] Sadly, the article is incomplete but it's still worth reading.
